# Knowledge synthesis

## Main patterns

The corpus shows four recurring conflict patterns:

1. **Water extraction and groundwater concern around large-scale brewery siting.** The Yucatán/Heineken case centers on Indigenous consultation, groundwater extraction, wastewater treatment trust, and community claims about water access.
2. **Wastewater and effluent management.** Government guidance and the Chhatarpur NGT report show that brewery wastewater can become a public-health, odor, sewer-corrosion, treatment-capacity, or water-contamination issue when treatment and pretreatment fail or are alleged to fail.
3. **Noise, entertainment, and land-use disputes around brewery taprooms.** The Carpinteria case illustrates how live music and taproom uses can generate municipal enforcement and nuisance disputes.
4. **Craft breweries, neighborhood change, and displacement risk.** Scholarly sources treat breweries as part of broader urban revitalization/gentrification dynamics. The strongest careful conclusion is not that breweries alone cause displacement, but that brewery siting can track and reinforce neighborhood change and therefore deserves planning attention.

## Affected communities

Affected groups include Maya communities and water-insecure households in Yucatán, village residents near alleged brewery discharge in Chhatarpur, residential neighbors near brewery entertainment uses in Carpinteria, and urban residents in neighborhoods experiencing revitalization and displacement risk.

## Strongest evidence

The strongest open evidence in this rapid corpus comes from government technical guidance on brewery wastewater mechanisms and open scholarly work on craft breweries and neighborhood change. News and tribunal reporting provide timely conflict signals but should be supplemented with legal filings, agency inspection reports, and community documents.

## Weak or uncertain evidence

Some records are metadata-only or news-only. Allegations about pollution, health impacts, consultation failures, and displacement should not be treated as confirmed causal findings without corroborating official records, full-text scholarly analysis, environmental monitoring data, or court documents.

## Multilingual additions

The multilingual extension strengthens Latin American coverage. Spanish-language sources add the Mexicali/Constellation Brands water conflict, a Spanish scholarly record about organized resistance and public consultation, and Argentine craft-brewery noise/odor disputes. Portuguese-language sources add a Porto Alegre craft-brewery neighborhood noise/legal conflict and the Brazilian Backer/Belorizontina craft-beer contamination case.

For non-English records, the corpus preserves original-language titles and short excerpts plus English equivalents. The English equivalents are for coding/navigation and should not replace the original sources for quotation or legal interpretation.

## Expanded European-language additions

The corpus now adds French, Dutch, German, and Italian records. Dutch records provide the strongest added conflict-specific evidence, including brewery-pub nuisance reporting and an administrative-law record concerning a beer-café operating permit and noise complaints. French and Italian records mainly strengthen water, effluent, and nightlife-noise context. A German record adds water-reuse and drought-adaptation context for brewing. These additions widen language coverage, but several are contextual rather than direct conflict cases and should be used cautiously.
